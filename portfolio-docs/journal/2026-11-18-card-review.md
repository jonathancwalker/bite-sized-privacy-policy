# Progressive reading and claim cards — November 18, 2026

The user redirected the prototype toward a bare, one-claim-at-a-time card: claim, progress, and Accept, Reject, Skip, or Reword. Original wording should stay available but not occupy the card. Priority can change the border; overview and reworded language should carry quiet cues so readers know to check the source. The user also clarified that Quick overview for an imported policy should mean far fewer cards and high-level themes that give an operating understanding, rather than merely a shorter paraphrase of the detailed review. These are product directions from the conversation, not a usability finding.

Before this iteration, the review screen placed a topic sidebar and original-source panel beside the claim, and user mode generated 21 Meta, 12 TikTok US, and 30 YouTube/Google cards. The saved [earlier claim capture](../assets/exports/2026-11-18-flow/claim.png) records that baseline. The implementation now uses a centered card, keeps the exact source behind Check source, advances after each reaction, records a per-claim AI rewording, and marks user-mode/reworded/priority states in the card treatment. Accept and Reject are personal reactions to a claim; the report still states that the review does not accept a policy or provide legal advice.

The reading-depth choice now appears progressively on the homepage after selecting a featured policy or expanding the own-policy path. Own-policy mode is sent to the live decomposition endpoint. Quick overview groups more source text before selecting at most two broad, grounded takeaways per group; detailed mode retains granular coverage. Saved featured overviews were regenerated from their previously fetched source snapshots, preserving the original fetch dates. This is a selective summary and may omit material clauses, which the overview and report disclose.

Local browser inspection confirmed the featured-policy mode reveal, own-policy reveal before Upload/Paste/URL, a responsive card at 390 pixels, Check source, a live rewording, and progress after Accept. The source modal still shows the full extracted policy, which keeps verification available but remains long to scan. This was implementation QA, not a reader study or legal review. Build and automated-test results are recorded in [experiments](../experiments.md); the running local API and hosted static prototype remain separate.
